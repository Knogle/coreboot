# B06WD live USB GPIO57/H_PWRGD experiment

Date: 2026-09-07

Status: immutable hardware research note.  This note records the retained
B06VP/B06WD CAR-ROMMON evidence.  It does not describe a B06WF target run and
does not qualify any automatic B06WF path.

## Result

The experiment establishes a causal board-level control that had been missing
from the B06WD boot path:

- changing GPIO56/`USB_MODE` from input to output while preserving its already
  high level was a negative control: every EHCI2 port remained `00003030`;
- reproducing the vendor-correlated GPIO57/`H_PWRGD` sequence -- output low,
  a bounded delay, then output high -- changed all six ports of each EHCI from
  `00003030` to `00003020`;
- in the corresponding UHCI view, live overcurrent-active cleared on all
  twelve companion ports.  D29 reported `0880` on all six ports.  D26 reported
  `0983`, `0880`, `0880`, `0880`, `0880`, `0883`; and
- an isolated, deliberately invasive D26:F0 port-1 sequence then completed in
  SeaBIOS-style register order: connected low-speed state `0983`, port-reset
  write `0200`, reset-active observation `0a82`, reset clear back to `0983`,
  port-enable write `0004`, and enabled observation `0987`.  The sealed program
  FNV was `bb167682`, and ROMMON reported `RUN=ok`.

This is strong causal evidence that GPIO57 is required to release the common
USB power-good/overcurrent path on this board.  It is not yet USB enumeration.
No device descriptor, USB address assignment, HID binding, accepted key, or
SeaBIOS USB-driver success is present in these captures.

## Fixed test context and safety boundary

The target was the retained B06WD CAR recovery ROMMON on the fixed MSI X58
Pro-E setup.  Controller identity, disabled decode, temporary exact BARs, GPIO
decode, GPIO ownership, direction, and initial level were admission gates in
the scripts.  One controller view was tested per isolated transaction.

Every invasive GPIO57 transaction required a **one-second AC interruption**
before another experiment.  That recovery step remains mandatory for any
repeat or successor test; a warm reset or ROMMON rollback is not an acceptable
substitute.  The retained recovery captures show a fresh B06WD CAR prompt
after the individual experiments.  A one-second mains interruption is the
defined laboratory procedure here; it is not a claim that electrical G3 was
independently instrumented.

No valuable USB storage should be attached while repeating a port-reset or
port-enable experiment.

## Register interpretation

### EHCI

The observed EHCI PORTSC transition was:

```text
before GPIO57 high: 00003030
after  GPIO57 high: 00003020
```

For the fields used by this experiment, `0x10` is live overcurrent active and
`0x20` is sticky overcurrent change.  Thus the transition clears the live OCA
field while retaining the historical OCC indication.  Bits `0x1000` and
`0x2000` remain set in both values.  The same transition occurred on ports
1--6 of D29:F7 (EHCI1) and ports 1--6 of D26:F7 (EHCI2).  The test therefore
observed the common OC0#-correlated state in both EHCI controller views; it
does not by itself identify a physical rear-panel connector or establish a
complete companion-port routing map.

### UHCI

The relevant ICH10 UHCI PORTSC fields are interpreted only to the extent
required by the experiment:

```text
bit  0  CCS   current connect status
bit  1  CSC   connect-status change
bit  2  PE    port enable
bit  8  LSDA  low-speed-device attached
bit  9  PR    port reset
bit 10  OCA   live overcurrent active
bit 11  OCI   sticky overcurrent indication/change
```

Consequently, baseline `0c80` includes live OCA and sticky OCI.  After GPIO57
went high, `0880` retained OCI but had OCA clear; `0983` additionally reported
CCS, CSC and LSDA; and `0883` reported CCS and CSC without LSDA.  The D26
attachment observations are evidence of electrical connect/line state only,
not of descriptor traffic or device identity.

### GP_LVL2 native-bit caveat

The bank-2 MSB-byte baseline at I/O `0x53b` was `15`.  GPIO57 is byte bit 1.
The script changed `GP_IO_SEL2` byte `0f -> 0d`, drove the GPIO57 latch high
(`15 -> 17` as written), and the following read returned `1f`.

Only byte bits 0 and 1 are selected as GPIO by
`GPIO_USE_SEL2=030300ff`; byte bit 3 is a native-function position.  Reads of
native-function bits through GP_LVL are not a reliable GPIO latch check.
Therefore the extra `0x08` in `1f` is a correlation with the released native
OC0# path, not proof that software explicitly wrote a second GPIO.  It also
explains the generic full-byte rollback verifier: after restoring the GPIO57
target bit low it could observe `1d` instead of the original `15`, even though
GPIO57 itself was low.  EHCI/UHCI live OCA clearing, not the native GP_LVL bit,
is the decisive result.

## GPIO56 negative control

The B06VP negative-control script preserved the level already sampled on
GPIO56: it changed the pin from input to output/high and waited for the short
bounded interval.  All six EHCI2 ports were `00003030` before and after the
change.  The transaction completed with program FNV `492f81ca`, and the
reversible register state was rolled back.

This rules out the narrow hypothesis that direction ownership plus the
already-high GPIO56 level alone clears overcurrent.  It does not prove that
GPIO56 is unrelated to USB, does not establish the electrical polarity of
`USB_MODE`, and does not authorize a GPIO56-low write.

## D26:F0 port-1 reset/enable transaction

The final invasive test used
[`ich10-h_pwrgd-gpio57-uhci4-port1-reset-enable-b06wd.xrs`](../scripts/ich10-h_pwrgd-gpio57-uhci4-port1-reset-enable-b06wd.xrs),
whose verified SHA-256 is
`058c831aac120e2f4d12bc07d70621d67a6523d043c1c8113a0e84ae6d1b49d4`.
It was sealed as program FNV `bb167682`.

The exact admitted sequence and observations were:

| Phase | Action or assertion | Observed result |
|---|---|---|
| Power-good release | GPIO57 output-low, delay, then output-high | D26:F0 port 1 `0983`; OCA clear, CCS and LSDA set |
| Reset assert | `outw PORTSC, 0200` | `0a82` after the reset-hold delay |
| Reset clear | `outw PORTSC, 0000` | `0983` |
| Enable | `outw PORTSC, 0004` | `0987`; PE asserted with CCS/LSDA retained |
| Companion observation | read D26:F0 port 2 | `0880` |
| Completion | ROMMON transaction status | `RUN=ok`, transaction FNV `a4390453` |

This ordering mirrors the essential SeaBIOS UHCI reset/enable progression,
but the XRS `delay` operand is **not time in microseconds or milliseconds**.
In this ROMMON backend, `delay 0x10000` means `0x10000` iterations of a loop
whose body executes `PAUSE`; it must not be described as 65 ms.  Likewise the
longer reset hold is a PAUSE count, not a calibrated USB-spec interval.  The
test proves the hardware state progression under that concrete loop, not USB
timing compliance.

The successful transcript is split:

- [`2026-09-07-b06wd-usb-car-gpio57-uhci4-port1-reset-load-v2.raw`](../../Documentation/PUBLIC_EVIDENCE.md#omitted-artifact-reference-index)
  records the complete program load/seal and FNV `bb167682`;
- [`2026-09-07-b06wd-usb-car-gpio57-uhci4-port1-reset-run-v2.raw`](../../Documentation/PUBLIC_EVIDENCE.md#omitted-artifact-reference-index)
  records the status and one-run unlock prefix with that FNV; and
- [`2026-09-07-b06wd-usb-car-recovery-before-uhci4-reset-v3.raw`](../../Documentation/PUBLIC_EVIDENCE.md#omitted-artifact-reference-index)
  records the complete retained end half from the reset-active read through
  `RUN=ok` and the final FNV/status line.

The output for operations 0--11 is not retained in the end-half raw file.
Their success is supported by the exact sealed script and the fact that the
interpreter reached later operations and ended `RUN=ok`; this is not presented
as one uninterrupted serial transcript.  The first version aborted safely at
its overly strict `0c80` precondition because it observed `0c82`, a sticky CSC
variation.  The revised program masked that asynchronous change bit without
weakening the OCA/CCS admission condition.

## Proven facts and non-claims

Proven for these concrete B06WD CAR transactions:

1. GPIO56 output/high alone did not change EHCI2 live overcurrent.
2. GPIO57 output-low/delay/high causally cleared live OCA in both EHCIs and all
   six UHCIs while the scripts' exact admission gates held.
3. D26 exposed two connected companion-port states after that release.
4. D26:F0 port 1 accepted the tested reset-clear-enable ordering and ended
   connected, low-speed and port-enabled (`0987`) with `RUN=ok`.
5. A one-second AC recovery returned the board to the CAR diagnostic path
   between invasive experiments.

Not proven:

- no descriptor was fetched and no USB address was assigned;
- no HID class device or keyboard input was established;
- no physical connector-to-controller map was established;
- no calibrated reset duration or USB timing compliance was measured;
- no automatic B06WF path, SeaBIOS enumeration, payload continuation, warm
  reset behavior, or ten-run stability milestone was tested; and
- the GPIO57 net name alone is not treated as a complete electrical schematic
  explanation.  The claim is limited to the measured OCA/PORTSC causality.

The highest-value successor is therefore an automatic but still gated
GPIO57-low/delay/high step followed by the already-instrumented SeaBIOS USB
path.  It should retain the one-second AC recovery rule and should judge the
next milestone by descriptor/control-transfer and accepted-key evidence, not
merely by OCA clearing.
